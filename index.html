The State of the League

Finding 01

The regular season is a rumor

The best team of a given season — by all-play, which can’t hide behind a schedule — has won the title 4 times in 13 years. Its most common finish is third: 5 bronzes to 4 rings.

Chirch won it all in 2016 at 6–7. Noodles won it all in 2020 at 6–7. And the bottom half of the all-time table holds 7 of the league’s 13 championships. The top half, where all the good teams live, holds 6.

Finding 02

Half your record isn’t yours

Across all 156 team-seasons, 49% of the spread in win totals is how you scored. 38% is who the schedule fed you. The rest is the two colliding.

And luck doesn’t stick: a manager’s luck one year predicts the next at r = 0.004. Nobody here is cursed. Not even Rhino, and he’s checked.

Finding 03

Every feud is one‑directional

Twelve managers, twelve worst enemies, and not a single pair points at each other — every rivalry in this league is unrequited. At the top it closes into a perfect loop: Avo’s worst enemy is Rhino. Rhino’s is Scorzo. Scorzo’s is Avo. Rock, paper, scissors, for thirteen years.

Each arrow points at the man who ruins him.

Finding 04

The margins are microscopic

33 of 1,226 games have been decided by less than a single point — two or three coin-flips a season. The closest: 2023, week 7. Wood 117.26, Avo 117.20. A margin of 0.06, and it counts forever.

Finding 05

Scoring has gone exactly nowhere

League average: 115.1 points a week in 2013, 113.1 in 2025. Thirteen years of draft software, waiver-wire vigils and 2,508 picks have moved weekly scoring two points. Backwards. The exceptions proved nothing: 2018 spiked to 122.2, 2017 sank to 108.5, and both snapped straight back.
